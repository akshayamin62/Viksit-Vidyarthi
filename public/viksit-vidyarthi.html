Viksit Vidyarthi for Viksit Bharat

A comprehensive educational development initiative that prepares students, empowers teachers, engages parents and strengthens institutions to contribute to the national vision of Viksit Bharat 2047.

“A developed nation begins with a developed student.”

India’s aspiration to become a developed nation by 2047 requires more than academic excellence. It needs young people who are intellectually capable, emotionally resilient, socially responsible, technologically adaptable and professionally competent.

Conventional approaches often weigh examination performance more heavily than the overall potential of students. This initiative bridges that gap by building a structured Student Development and Career Readiness Ecosystem inside schools and colleges.

Why this initiative matters

India needs young people who can turn education into competence, character, contribution and meaningful careers. These challenges stand in the way.

Student & parent

Gap between achievement and readiness

Students may do well in exams yet struggle with critical thinking, problem-solving, communication and applying knowledge.

The shift: from exam-oriented education to learning-oriented development.

Student & parent

Lack of early career planning

Many students choose streams, subjects and careers without awareness of their abilities, interests or opportunities.

The shift: informed, structured exploration from the middle-school years.

Student & parent

Digital distraction

Excess recreational screen time and fragmented attention can undermine disciplined learning.

The shift: digital responsibility and self-regulation, not just restriction.

Educator

Changing classroom dynamics

Teachers face diverse learning preferences, behavioural challenges and evolving student–teacher relationships.

The shift: engaging contemporary learners while keeping discipline and mutual respect.

Student & parent

Parenting in a changing world

Parents are unsure about academic choices, emerging careers, peer influence and technology.

The shift: from pressure-driven supervision to informed developmental guidance.

Educator

Employability and future skills

Qualifications alone do not guarantee competence. Industries need technological literacy, communication, creativity, collaboration and continuous learning.

The shift: career readiness well before the job market.

Why institutions must take the lead. Schools and colleges reach students in their most formative years. Addressing these challenges systematically lets them become centres of holistic development, complementing the aims of India’s National Education Policy 2020: holistic development, experiential learning, critical thinking and multidisciplinary education.
